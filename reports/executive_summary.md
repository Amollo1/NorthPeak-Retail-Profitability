# Executive Summary
## NorthPeak Retail — Profitability Decline Analysis

**Prepared for:** Executive Leadership, Finance, Sales, and Regional Management
**Prepared by:** Benard Onyango Omoga, Business/Data Analyst
**Date:** September 2026

---

### The Situation

NorthPeak Retail's sales have grown steadily over the past three years — up **22%** since 2023 — but profit has not kept pace. Overall profit margin has fallen from roughly **19%** in early 2023 to just **12.5%** by the end of 2025, and finance data confirms this is a genuine, sustained decline rather than normal month-to-month variation.

In plain terms: **the business is selling more but keeping less of every shilling earned.**

---

### What's Driving the Decline

A detailed analysis of three years of sales, cost, and discount data — covering all products, regions, and customer types — identified three specific, addressable causes.

**1. A pricing problem in one segment, not the whole business**
Corporate customers now account for a third of all sales (up from 27%), but their share of *profit* has fallen to just 10%. Corporate deals are being discounted far more heavily than other customer types, and that gap has widened every year. In 2025, Corporate sales overall earned just 4.6% margin — compared to roughly 20% from Consumer and Home Office customers.

**2. Rising discounts in one region and category**
Furniture sales in the Nairobi region show a discount rate that has climbed from about 9% to over 28% over the past 18 months — far faster than the same category anywhere else in the business. This single pattern is a major contributor to Nairobi being the lowest-margin region in the company today.

**3. A product line that has never been profitable**
The "Tables" product line (conference tables, study tables, pantry tables) has run at a **loss** for the entire three-year period — not because of recent discounting, but because its cost structure was too thin from the start. This is a separate, standing issue layered on top of the recent decline.

---

### Why This Matters

If left unaddressed, these three issues will continue to erode profit even as sales keep growing — meaning more revenue increasingly buys less actual profit for the business. Because each cause is specific and measurable, each is also directly fixable without requiring a slowdown in sales growth.

---

### Recommended Next Steps

1. **Introduce discount approval thresholds for Corporate accounts**, tied to the specific deal size and product category, rather than open-ended negotiation.
2. **Review and cap Furniture discounting in the Nairobi region** specifically, restoring it toward the level seen in other regions.
3. **Re-price or reformulate the Tables product line**, or reposition it as a lower-priority category, given it has never generated profit.
4. **Establish a monthly profitability dashboard** (in development) so discount and margin issues are visible to decision-makers before they compound over multiple quarters, rather than being discovered at year-end.

Full detail, supporting data, and quantified impact estimates for each recommendation are provided in the accompanying **Business Recommendations** report.

---

### Bottom Line

NorthPeak does not have a sales problem — it has a **margin management problem**, concentrated in three specific, identifiable areas. Addressing them does not require slowing growth; it requires tighter control over how that growth is priced and discounted.
